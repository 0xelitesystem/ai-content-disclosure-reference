# When disclosure is required

Disclosure is required or strongly expected in a growing set of cases: where rules like the FTC guidance, California AB 2013, and the EU AI Act apply, and wherever presenting AI work as human would mislead. The specifics vary by jurisdiction and use, so the safe approach is to disclose whenever AI materially shaped the content.

## The regulatory landscape in general terms

Several frameworks now touch AI disclosure. Consumer-protection guidance, such as that from the FTC, treats misleading claims about content and endorsements as a problem, which can reach undisclosed AI use that deceives. State laws, such as California AB 2013, introduce transparency requirements around AI. The EU AI Act sets obligations that increase with risk, including disclosure expectations for certain AI-generated or AI-manipulated content. The common thread is a move toward requiring transparency.

## High-sensitivity uses

Disclosure expectations are strongest where the stakes for the audience are higher: content that could be mistaken for a real person, AI-generated likenesses or voices, AI standing in for professional advice, and political or news content where authenticity matters. In these areas, undisclosed AI is most likely to mislead and most likely to be regulated. If your use touches any of them, treat disclosure as required and confirm the specifics.

## Endorsements and testimonials

Where AI is used in marketing, endorsements, or reviews, disclosure rules around honesty and material connections apply. Presenting AI-generated praise as a genuine human testimonial, or hiding that content is AI-made when that fact would matter to the audience, runs against endorsement guidance. The principle is that the audience should not be misled about what they are looking at or who, or what, produced it.

## Why the safe approach is to disclose

Because the rules differ by jurisdiction and use, and because they are changing, working out the exact line for every piece of content is impractical and risky. Disclosing whenever AI materially shaped the content sidesteps the analysis: if you always disclose meaningful AI involvement, you are unlikely to fall foul of a requirement to disclose it. For the edge cases where real legal weight is involved, confirm with a professional rather than guessing.
