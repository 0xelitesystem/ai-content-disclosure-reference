# AI Content Disclosure

A plain-language reference on disclosing AI-generated content: when disclosure is required or expected, how rules like the FTC guidance, California AB 2013, and the EU AI Act treat it, and how to disclose honestly without overcomplicating it. Written for creators and operators publishing AI-assisted work.

**Not legal advice.** Disclosure rules vary by jurisdiction and use, and they are changing. This explains the landscape so you know what to watch for. It does not tell you what your specific obligation is. Confirm with a qualified professional for your situation.

## The core idea

Disclosing AI-generated content means telling your audience when content was created or substantially assisted by AI, rather than presenting it as human-authored. The honest default is to disclose, because trust depends on it and because disclosure rules are tightening across jurisdictions. Some uses now carry legal disclosure requirements; many more carry an ethical and reputational expectation.

## What is inside

- `01-why-disclose.md` the trust and legal reasons.
- `02-when-disclosure-is-required.md` the rules taking shape.
- `03-how-to-disclose.md` doing it clearly and proportionately.
- `04-what-not-to-do.md` the failures that break trust or rules.
- `05-a-practical-policy.md` a simple disclosure policy to adopt.

## The stance

This reference treats honesty as the safe default. When in doubt, disclose. The cost of disclosing is small; the cost of being caught passing AI work off as human, whether by an audience or a regulator, is much larger.
