# A practical policy

Adopt a simple, consistent disclosure policy: disclose whenever AI materially shaped the content, place the disclosure where the audience will see it, describe AI's role honestly, and apply the same approach every time. Confirm the specifics for any high-stakes or regulated use.

## A policy you can actually follow

A workable policy has a few rules and no exceptions you have to think hard about. Disclose when AI did a meaningful part of the work. Put the note where someone taking in the content will find it. Say honestly what AI did. Use the same wording and placement consistently. Skip prominent labels only for trivial assistance like spell-check. A policy this simple is one you will actually apply, which is what makes it work.

## Write it down

Putting the policy in writing, even briefly, makes it consistent across your content and over time, and it gives you something to point to. A short internal note that states when you disclose, where, and how is enough. Consistency is easier when the rule exists somewhere other than your memory, and a written policy is also easier to defend if anyone questions your practice.

## Build it into the workflow

Disclosure sticks when it is part of how you publish, not an afterthought. Add the disclosure step to your publishing checklist so it happens every time a relevant piece goes out. Building it into the workflow means you do not have to remember it case by case, and you do not discover after publishing that a label was missing. The habit, embedded in the process, is what keeps the policy real.

## Get help for the hard cases

For uses that carry real legal weight, such as AI likenesses, AI standing in for professional advice, political or news content, or anything in a heavily regulated area, a general policy is not enough. These are the cases to confirm with a qualified professional, because the specific requirements vary and the consequences of getting them wrong are higher. The everyday policy handles the routine; professional advice handles the edge. Knowing which is which is part of the policy.
