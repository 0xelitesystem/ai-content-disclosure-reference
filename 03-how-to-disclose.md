# How to disclose

Disclose AI involvement clearly, honestly, and in proportion to how much AI shaped the content. A short, plain statement where the audience will see it is enough; the disclosure should be findable, not buried, and accurate about the role AI played.

## Be clear and findable

A disclosure works only if the audience can find it. Place it where someone encountering the content would reasonably see it, not hidden in fine print far from the content itself. A brief, plain-language note that the content was created or assisted by AI, in a visible spot, does the job. The test is whether a reasonable person taking in the content would come away knowing AI was involved.

## Be honest about the role

Match the disclosure to what actually happened. Content generated mostly by AI, content where AI assisted a human author, and content where AI did a small part are different, and the disclosure can reflect that honestly. Overstating or understating AI's role both mislead. A truthful description of how AI was used is both more honest and more useful to the audience than a vague catch-all.

## Keep it proportionate

Disclosure should be proportionate, not overwhelming. A meaningful AI role deserves a clear note; a trivial use, such as a grammar check, generally does not need a prominent label. The goal is to inform the audience about what matters, not to plaster every piece with disclaimers until they become noise. Judgment about what materially shaped the content guides how prominent the disclosure needs to be.

## Consistency builds trust

Disclosing consistently, the same way across your content, makes the disclosure credible and easy for your audience to understand. A predictable, honest label becomes part of how your audience reads your work, rather than a surprise. Consistency also protects you, because a steady practice of disclosure is easier to defend than ad hoc decisions made differently each time. Decide your approach once and apply it the same way.
