# What not to do

Do not present AI-generated content as human-authored, do not hide disclosure where no one will find it, do not fabricate AI-generated testimonials or reviews, and do not overstate or understate AI's role. These are the failures that break audience trust or run afoul of disclosure rules.

## Passing AI work off as human

The central failure is presenting content as written by a person when it was generated by AI, in a context where that distinction matters to the audience. This is the deception that disclosure rules and audience trust both target. Whatever the disclosure approach, the line not to cross is actively misrepresenting AI work as human. Honesty about the source is the floor.

## Burying the disclosure

A disclosure that exists but is hidden where no one will see it is close to no disclosure at all. Placing a label in fine print far from the content, or in a spot the audience will not reach, defeats the purpose and can fail a requirement that disclosure be clear and conspicuous. If you disclose, make it findable. A technically present but practically invisible disclosure protects neither trust nor compliance.

## Fabricating AI testimonials

Using AI to generate fake reviews, testimonials, or endorsements and presenting them as genuine is a serious failure on both ethical and regulatory grounds. It combines deception about the source with deception about the endorsement itself. Endorsement and consumer-protection rules treat fabricated or undisclosed material connections as violations. AI does not make a fake testimonial acceptable; it makes one easier to produce, which is exactly why the rule against them matters more.

## Misstating the role

Both overstating and understating AI's involvement mislead. Claiming content is fully human when AI did most of it understates AI; claiming heavy AI authorship when a human did the real work overstates it, sometimes to dodge accountability for the content. The honest path is to describe what actually happened. Misstating the role in either direction undermines the trust that disclosure is supposed to build.
